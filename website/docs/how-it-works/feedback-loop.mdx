# Feedback Loop

Aegis does not rely on static, hand-tuned scoring weights. Instead, it operates a continuous feedback loop that refines ranking exponents over time based on real-world evidence. The system learns from two sources: expert audit panels and downstream task quality.

## Downstream Task Quality

When Aegis recommends candidates for a labeling task, the resulting work product has measurable quality. Three signals are ingested back into Aegis:

- **Fleiss kappa:** A measure of inter-rater agreement among the researchers assigned to a task. High kappa indicates that the recommended researchers produced consistent, reliable labels. Low kappa suggests the researchers may not have been well-matched to the task.
- **Accept rate:** The proportion of completed labels that the customer accepted without revision. A high accept rate indicates that the recommended candidates performed well.
- **Consensus rate:** The proportion of labels where all assigned researchers agreed. This captures the degree of alignment among the recommended candidates on a given task.

These signals are mapped back to the specific (query, candidate) pairs that produced them. Within each task, candidates who produced higher-quality outputs are ranked above those who produced lower-quality outputs, creating derived pairwise judgments that feed directly into weight learning.

## Weight Relearning

The Plackett-Luce model is refit on a weekly cadence using the growing union of two data sources:

- **Audit-panel judgments:** Expert pairwise comparisons collected during Phase 1, where domain experts directly compared pairs of candidates and indicated which should rank higher.
- **Downstream-quality-derived pairs:** Pairwise judgments derived from task outcomes as described above.

As downstream data accumulates and proves its reliability, it receives higher weight in the fitting process. This reflects the fact that real-world task outcomes are a more direct measure of ranking quality than retrospective expert judgments, once enough data has been collected.

The training set grows monotonically — new judgments are added but old ones are never removed. Over time, per-specialty exponents naturally diverge as different specialties accumulate different evidence distributions. A specialty with abundant downstream feedback may see its weights shift substantially from the cold-start values, while a specialty with limited data remains closer to the audit-panel-derived baseline.

## Cold Start

When Aegis first launches or enters a new specialty, there is no downstream data to learn from. The cold-start strategy uses prior weights derived from audit-panel judgments. A cold-start guard prevents the learning pipeline from fitting weights when the judgment count is below a minimum threshold, ensuring that noisy early data does not produce unstable rankings. Until the threshold is met, the system uses the most recent stable weight vector.

## Guardrails

The feedback loop includes several safeguards to prevent bad data or edge cases from degrading ranking quality:

- **Convergence checks:** Each refit must converge within defined tolerances. If the optimizer fails to converge, the refit is discarded and the prior weights are retained.
- **Stability monitoring:** The system tracks weight drift across refit cycles. If weights shift too far too fast, an alert fires and the change is flagged for review rather than auto-deployed.
- **Auto-deploy vs. manual gate:** Small weight changes (where all exponent deltas are below a threshold) are deployed automatically. Large changes require manual approval from a human reviewer before taking effect. This prevents a sudden influx of anomalous feedback from silently changing rankings.
- **Per-specialty coverage tracking:** The system monitors how many judgments are available per specialty. Specialties with thin data are flagged, and their weights receive lower confidence, ensuring that poorly-covered specialties do not produce overconfident rankings.
- **Refit reports:** Every refit cycle produces a detailed report comparing prior and new weights, per-specialty judgment coverage, confidence intervals, source breakdown (audit-panel vs. downstream), and the deployment decision. These reports create an audit trail for understanding how and why weights changed over time.
